- I am an active learner with a strong passion for exploring new technologies, tools, and data-driven approaches to problem-solving.

- I recently completed my PhD in Computer Science at the University of Lethbridge, where my research examined how sociolinguistic factors—such as gender, region, and programming expertise—influence coding styles and code quality. My work involved analyzing over 700,000 C++ programs and applying machine learning models (Random Forest, BERT, CodeBERT, and T5) to classify programmer groups, assess code quality, and provide actionable feedback. As part of my contributions, I developed a recommendation model that generates metric-driven guidance to improve code quality, and built a code-insight tool that integrates classification results, quality assessments, and improvement suggestions to support learners and researchers. This research was funded by the Alberta Innovates Graduate Scholarship ($31,000/year for four years).

- Before my doctoral studies, I completed my M.Sc. in Computer Science, specializing in Natural Language Processing (NLP). My master’s work focused on query-focused summarization using the BERT-SUM model, and I published the results at INLG, a conference under the ACL. I also have hands-on experience with a wide range of ML architectures, including BERT, BART, Pegasus, T5, GPT models, LED, XLNet, and graph neural networks such as GNN and GAT.

- Beyond research, I worked as a software developer, contributing to .NET-based applications and participating in collaborative projects including Mitacs research initiatives and the development of a University Management System. These experiences strengthened my teamwork, project design, and full-stack development capabilities.

- As a Lecturer at the University of Lethbridge, I gained valuable experience teaching and mentoring students from diverse cultural and academic backgrounds. I am committed to helping learners grow as critical thinkers and problem solvers through supportive and engaging instruction.
